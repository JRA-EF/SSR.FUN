Architecture scope (original 3-surface framing)

Three surfaces evaluated independently to prevent optional integrations from obscuring protocol readiness.

1. Core protocol

Reserve creation, Reserve deposits and redemptions, Reserve Token accounting, governance, permissions, fees, lifecycle controls and program security.

2. Jupiter routing

Optional execution path for acquiring or disposing of Reserves. Quote integrity, slippage, transaction construction, failure recovery and degraded-mode behavior.

3. Secondary market

Optional liquidity for Reserve Tokens. Pool creation and market trading are not prerequisites for proportional protocol minting and redemption unless formally made so.

Evidence standard. Compiling code, a green build, source inspection, mocked tests or local simulations do not prove live functionality. They may support a status of Partial. Passed requires the acceptance criterion to be executed against the named deployment, with reproducible evidence and expected state changes.

Integrations — what's needed for Mainnet

Four things stand between the current DevNet setup and a real closed Mainnet. Alternatives are the 2-3 biggest players in each category as of mid-2026 — worth a quick compare before committing.

Not integrated at all

Swap routing — Jupiter

Replaces the DevNet swap authority above with real execution and real liquidity. Note: Jupiter itself has zero DevNet awareness (confirmed by a real API test in this repo's history) — this can only be proven on Mainnet, which is exactly why it's on the speedrun list.

Jupiter is the default (~90% of Solana DEX-aggregation volume, reportedly). Alternatives:
  • OKX DEX — multi-chain aggregator incl. Solana, reported ~$300–450M/day Solana volume, CEX+DEX hybrid quotes.
  • dFlow — auction-based routing, MEV protection by design (fills come from competing market makers, not a public mempool).
Not integrated at all

Price oracle — Pyth

Every USD-denominated number on screen (NAV, AUM, price) is a hardcoded test constant today. Core mint/redeem math itself doesn't need a price feed — this is specifically a "the numbers on screen are fake" gap, not a correctness gap.

Pyth is the natural default (Solana-native, sub-second feeds). Alternatives:
  • Chainlink — overall oracle market leader (reportedly ~70% share across chains), has Solana feeds, most widely trusted by integrators.
  • Switchboard — Solana-native alternative, long track record on this exact chain, permissionless feed creation.
Single dev key today

Upgrade authority — Multisig

The program's upgrade authority (and the reused swap/mint-authority key) is one person's keypair. Before real funds are at risk, this moves to a multisig.

Recommendation:
  • Squads — the dominant Solana-native multisig/treasury product; this is the default choice, not really a 3-way market decision the way Jupiter/Pyth are.
DevNet tier only

Production RPC — Helius Mainnet

Same provider already trusted for DevNet, just the paid Mainnet-tier plan instead of the DevNet key currently configured.

If comparing:
  • Triton One — established, performance-focused Solana RPC infrastructure.
  • QuickNode — large multi-chain RPC provider with a mature Solana offering.
